# Research notes: what to borrow, what to avoid

Summary of a review (October 2026) of popular English-learning apps and of second-language-acquisition
research, used to design EnglishLab. App strengths and weaknesses come mostly from reviews, store pages
and company posts, so treat them as consistent signals rather than measured results.

## Apps

| App | Borrowed | Avoided |
|---|---|---|
| Speak | High speaking volume, role-play built from the learner's own situation, mistake review after each conversation | Repetitive content at higher levels, shallow vocabulary tracking |
| Duolingo | Tiny minimum to keep the streak (one session), up to two streak freezes, a tutor that remembers past calls | XP and leagues that reward volume over difficulty |
| 말해보카 | Placement test, explanation for every wrong answer, forgetting-curve review | Recognition-only multiple choice (plateaus at intermediate) |
| ELSA | Specific pronunciation feedback | Over-strict scoring; judge intelligibility instead |
| Cake | Expression-first learning from real spoken English | Fragmented exposure without long-form input |
| LingQ | Known-word database, "you know ~96 % of this text", context-based vocabulary | Learner must find level-appropriate content alone; review debt |
| Anki | FSRS scheduler with a retention target and daily caps | Card-making effort, out-of-context cards |
| Language Reactor / Readlang | Line-by-line intensive listening, phrase-level lookup, cards keep their sentence | Dual subtitles as a permanent crutch |
| Praktika / Loora | Always-available, low-anxiety speaking with a feedback report | AI pitched at the wrong level, robotic or too polite (no corrections) |
| Ringle | Accuracy / complexity / fluency / pronunciation (CAF) report, top-3 focus areas, errors linked to sentences | Cost of human tutors |
| TOEFL tools | Task-type practice and band estimates | Teaching test tricks instead of ability |

## Principles from research

- **Comprehensible input and coverage.** Native TV needs roughly 3,000 word families (plus proper nouns) for
  95 % coverage and 6,000–7,000 for 98 %; unscripted shows somewhat less. Reaching C1–C2 takes a large
  volume of extensive reading and listening (EnglishLab tracks input hours towards 1,000).
- **Spaced retrieval** with FSRS needs fewer reviews than SM-2 at the same retention.
- **Output with corrective feedback** has durable effects; prompts that make the learner self-correct tend to
  beat simply giving the corrected form (EnglishLab: nudge + retry during the conversation, full corrections
  in the report).
- **Shadowing** improves bottom-up listening and prosody; **narrow listening** (same topic or speaker)
  recycles vocabulary; **formulaic chunks** drive fluency (cards favour collocations and phrases).
- **Deliberate practice of weaknesses:** recurring error patterns become correction cards and focus areas.
- **Gamification** has only small effects; reward time on challenging material, not points.

## TOEFL iBT (since 21 January 2026)

- Sections: Reading (Complete the Words, Read in Daily Life, Read an Academic Passage), Listening (Listen and
  Choose a Response, Conversation, Announcement, Academic Talk), Writing (Build a Sentence, Write an Email,
  Academic Discussion), Speaking (Listen and Repeat, Take an Interview). About 90 minutes of base time;
  Reading and Listening are multistage adaptive.
- Each section is scored 1–6 in half bands; the overall score is the average. Band 6 = C2, 5–5.5 = C1,
  4–4.5 = B2 (ETS). A comparable 0–120 score is reported until about January 2028.
- The new tasks overlap with general training (Listen and Repeat ≈ shadowing, Take an Interview ≈ spontaneous
  conversation), so TOEFL-specific practice should stay around 20–30 % of study time until the last
  8–10 weeks before the test.
